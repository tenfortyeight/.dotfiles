# Prose that reads like a person wrote it

The reader came for something. Give it to them early, say it once, and stop when they have it.
Write as one capable colleague to another: warm, plain, and confident because the facts are.

## Shape

- Start with the summary: what this is and what the reader can do with it. A reader who stops
  after the first paragraph should still have the point.
- Go deeper only where the reader needs it to act or to avoid a mistake. Leave out what they
  would skim past.
- Say each thing once. No opening that announces what the sections will say, and no closing
  that repeats them.
- Use paragraphs for reasoning, lists for things a reader scans or follows in order, and a
  table only to compare.

## Voice

- Make the actor the subject: "the script deletes the branch", not "the branch is deleted".
- Use plain actions instead of labels: "pickers can't start until 20 minutes before", not "the
  picking-window constraint".
- Let the facts carry the confidence. Drop intensifiers ("really", "truly", "every single",
  "critical") and stacked hedges.
- Don't tell the reader how to feel or how easy it is: no "simply", "just", "obviously", "it's
  important to note".
- Use only the facts you were given. If you work something out (a sum, an estimate, a
  likely cause), say that you did, or leave it out.
- Hand a decision over once, with the decision inside the question: "Shall we drop the
  30-minute window?" rather than "It's your call. What do you think?"

## Habits to cut

| Habit | Why it grates |
|---|---|
| "Good question", "Great point" | grades the person who asked |
| "Short answer: … Here's why:" | announces the answer instead of giving it |
| "The real question is…" | corrects the asker before answering them |
| "It's your call" before a question | hands the decision over twice |
| Bold labels like "Decision needed:", "Bottom line:", "Key takeaway:" | repeat the sentence that follows |
| "It's worth noting", "One separate thing I noticed along the way" | throat-clearing before the point |
| "Not X, but Y" set up only to knock X down | argues with something nobody said |
| Three adjectives or three parallel clauses where one does | rhythm instead of meaning |
| "Let's dive in", "Whether you're X or Y", "In today's…" | brochure voice |

## Before handing it over

Read it as the reader, once. Cut each sentence they would skip. Check that the first paragraph
alone carries the point.

For narration, write for the ear: shorter sentences, no brackets or lists, numbers as they are
said.
